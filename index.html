What can you test?

Experiment A: Intermittent scarcity. Keep food availability constant and raise fasting probability. Observe how reserves, deaths and inherited metabolic efficiency evolve across many generations.

Experiment B: The famine shock. Run 150 days at baseline, then trigger a 30-day famine. Compare survivors' traits with the starting population. Repeat after resetting: chance affects individual runs.

Experiment C: Movement versus survival. Increase movement demands during scarcity. Does selection favour energy efficiency, reduced movement or stronger foraging?

Important distinction: Organisms have an individual short-term acclimation response and inherited traits that change through reproduction. The mechanisms and parameter values are invented for exploration; this is not a validated model of human fasting, metabolism, evolution or medical outcomes.

All simulation data remains in this page. No accounts, tracking or external libraries. Closing the browser resets the simulation; export CSV to preserve results.
